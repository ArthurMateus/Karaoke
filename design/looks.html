Four looks for Karaoke Party

The same four screens in each style: the home page, a room lobby, the song library, and the singing screen coming in Milestone 3. They're mockups with sample content, not the working app. Pick one, or mix parts from several, and I'll restyle the real app to match.

A · Neon Booth B · Mixtape C · Bandeirinha D · Pitch Lane

Look ANeon Booth

A late-night karaoke box: dark violet walls, pink and cyan neon tubes, a marquee logo. Big glowing lyrics are easy to read from across a dim room or on a TV.

Type
Monoton (logo), Bungee (headings), Rubik (text)
Best for
Parties at night, playing on a TV, a younger crowd
Colors

Look BMixtape

A 70s cassette and songbook: mustard yellow, burnt-orange stripes, cards like tape labels with typewriter text and hard-edged shadows. Warm and a bit cheeky.

Type
Shrikhand (headings), Karla (text), Space Mono (codes and buttons)
Best for
Standing out, a nostalgic and playful brand
Colors

Look CBandeirinha

A festa junina in the backyard: blue sky, strings of colorful paper flags, chunky friendly type and big rounded buttons. Cheerful and very Brazilian.

Type
Chango (headings), Baloo 2 (text)
Best for
Families and casual play, the Brazilian audience
Colors

Look DPitch Lane

Clean and modern on bright paper, with one strong violet. Its signature is the note lane itself: piano-roll bars appear on the home page and become the stage when singing.

Type
Unbounded (headings), Figtree (text)
Best for
Looking polished and trustworthy, a premium product feel
Colors

How they differ

Neon Booth feels like a karaoke bar at night and is the easiest to read in a dark room. Mixtape is warm and retro and stands out the most. Bandeirinha leans into Brazilian party culture (festa junina flags) and suits family and casual play. Pitch Lane is the cleanest and most "app-like", and it makes the note lane, the heart of the game, the brand's signature.
